REVIEW OF BIS ANNUAL REPORT 2020-21
Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), the National Standards Body of India was established under the BIS
Act 1986, assuming the functions of the erstwhile Indian Standards Institution (ISI). BIS is responsible
for the harmonious development of the activities of standardization, conformity assessment and quality
assurance of goods, articles, processes, systems and services and for matters connected therewith or
incidental thereto.
The Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016, was published for general infomiation in official gazette on
22 March 2016 and came into force on 12 October 2017.
Major achievements of BIS during the year 2020-21, as reported in its Annual Report are summarized
below:
STANDARDIZATION
The process of standards development adopted by BIS follows internationally accepted good
standardization practices and is in compliance with the WTO-TBT code of good practice for
preparation, adoption and application of standards. In the development of National Standards, BIS
provides all interested stakeholders, with a neutral platform to come together and work towards common
agreements. BIS provides a forum for Sectional Committees, representing over a thousand experts from
organized consumers and consumer bodies, regulatory and other government bodies, industries,
scientists, technologists, testing organizations and individual experts. These committees work to develop
consensus based voluntary National standards with stakeholder consultations.
During 2020-21. 992 (554 new and 438 revised) standards were published. The total number of
standards in force, as on 31 March 2021 was 21901.

Rules framed there-underand BIS (Conformity Assessment) Regulations. 2018. Under this Certification
scheme, a manufacturer of a particular product is given the self-marking rights of the BIS standards
mark (ISl mark) on his product after ensuring that his product complies with the relevant Indian
Standard. Presence of Standard Mark (popularly known as ISI mark) on a product indicates its
conformity to the relevant Indian Standard. The certification scheme is voluntary in nature, except for
361 product standards that have been made mandatory by the Central Government on considerations of
health and safety of the consumer.
During the 2020-21, 4878 new licences were granted, which include 55 products covered for the first
time under the scheme. The total number of Indian Standards which have been covered so far under BIS
Certification Marks Scheme up to March 2021 was 1033. The total number of operative licences held by
domestic manufacturers as on 31 March 2021 was 37873.
In order to monitor the operation of licences, during the year, a total number of 13632 inspections were
carried out.
Foreign Manufactures Certification Scheme (FMCS)
BIS has been operating separate Scheme for foreign manufacturers. Under this Scheme, foreign
manufacturers can seek certification from BIS for use of BIS Standard Mark on their product(s). During
2020-21, 24 licences were issued under FMCS, taking the total number of operative licences to 1009
against 119 Indian Standards. The licences granted covered products such as steel & steel products;
cement: PVC insulated cables; tyres & 
tubes for automotive vehicles; plastic feeding bottles; switchgear
products; plugs & socket-outlets and switches; HDPE & UPVC Pipes; Infant milk formula; ac static
energy meters etc. from about 53 countries.
Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS)
The Compulsory Registration Scheme is being operated by BIS under the Scheme - 
II of Schedule - 
II
of the BIS (Conformity Assessment) Regulations. 2018.The introduction of CRS is an alternative
mechanism to the Compulsory Certification to facilitate growth of fast growing sectors like IT and
protect consumers from spurious and sub-standard products manufactured in India or abroad.
The first licence was granted by BIS in June 2013. As on 31 March 2021, BIS has 16650 operative
licences in various countries including domestic manufacturers.
LABORATORY SERVICES
One of the main pillars of conformity assessment is product testing to ascertain the conformity of
products to relevant standards. BIS has established eight laboratories in the country to cater to the testing
need of samples generated from conformity assessment schemes, beginning with the establishment of
Central Laboratory at Sahibabad in 1962. Subsequently, four regional laboratories at Mohali, Kolkala.
Mumbai and Chennai and three branch office laboratories at Patna. Banualore and Guwahati were

--- Page 14 ---
established. BIS laboratories have facilities for testing of products in the field of chemical,
microbiological, electrical and mechanical requirements. In addition to product testing, BIS has
established gold assaying lab at Chennai, Sahibabad and Kolkata. Gold assaying lab at Chennai is a
referral lab and also tests silver jewellery.
BIS labs have complete test facilities for 398 Indian Standards and in addition, partial test facilities are
also available for 320 Indian Standards. During the year 2020-21, the BIS laboratories have tested
12354 samples of various products covered under Certification.
Lab Recognition Scheme
BIS has established Laboratory Recognition Scheme (LRS) for recognition of outside laboratories
(OSLs). The scheme is based on International Standard IS/ISO/IEC 17025, and in line with the criteria
adopted by the National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (NABL) for
accreditation of laboratories.
During the year, 24 new labs were recognized. As on 31 March 2021, the total no. of operative BIS
recognized labs were 267, which include reputed R&D organizations, technical institutions, Govt. labs
and Private sector labs. Besides this, services of 258 Government laboratories of specialized nature are
utilized for different products, as and when required.
Quality Assurance Activities
Quality Assurance is a regular part of testing in BIS Labs by way of which the quality of testing is
assured. During the year, 549 Samples were tested under Quality Assurance activities by BIS Labs.
These also include the samples tested during participation in proficiency testing/ inter-lab comparison
program.
Response to COVrD-19 Pandemic
In view of the urgent requirement of test facilities for PPEs in the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, BIS
labs proactively responded towards creation of the test facilities. Complete test facilities for Coverall for
COVID-19 has been created in three BIS Labs and complete test facilities for face Shields and partial
test facilities for Filtering Half Masks, Surgical Masks and eye protectors were created in Central
Laboratory, Sahibabad. Recognition of OSLs was carried out on priority basis for PPE testing and
Government Laboratories having testing facilities were also empanelled by BIS for testing of PPEs.
Further, to address the difficulties expressed by various recognized OSLs, the validity of recognition of
labs was extended up to 31 December, 2020.
Creation of New Testing Facilities in BIS Laboratories
Test facilities for Toys, Protective Helmet for two wheeler rides, Plugs, sockets and switches. Shot
Firing Cables, Cashew kernels, Electric iron, electric stoves and electric kitchen machines, low heat
Portland cement, masonry cement, super sulphated cement, rapid hardening portland cement, oil well
cement and high alumina cement for structural use were created.

HALLMARKING
Hallmarking of gold jewellery was launched by BIS in April 2000 to provide third party assurance to
consumers on the purity of gold jewellery or its fineness. The scheme for Hallmarking of silver
jewellery/ artefacts was launched in October 2005. Under these Scheme, while the jewellers are granted
licence to sell hallmarked jewellery, Assaying & Hallmarking (A&H) centres are recognized to assay
the purity of the jewellery submitted by the licensed jeweller, along with declaration of purity.
As on 31 March 2021, the number of operative licences for Hallmarking of gold and silver jewellery
was 34647.The number of operative BIS recognized A&H centres was 945. During the year, 2.47 crore
articles of gold and silver jewellery/ artefacts were hallmarked.
Hallmark of Gold Bullion/Coin
Hallmarking of gold Bullion/Coin in the fineness of 999 & 
995 by giving licence to refineries/Mint was
started in October 2015. As on 31 March 2021, 41 licences have been granted to refineries/ India
Government Mint for Gold bullion and coin.
Gold Monetization Scheme
Government of India has launched Gold Monetization Scheme on 05 November 2015. BIS has played
an important role in finalization and implementation of the Gold Monetization Scheme in association
with Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance and Reserve Bank of India. Under the
scheme, A&H Centres recognized by BIS have been qualified to act as Collection and Purity Testing
Centres (CPTCs). So far, 48 A&H centres and one Jeweller have been qualified to act as CPTC. The
gold collected by CPTCs is to be refined by refineries licenced by BIS.
Promotion of Hallmarking
To promote hallmarking in the country and for effective consumer protection, awareness programmes
are organized by BIS through its various Regional and Branch offices across the country. During 2020-
21, 74 such jewellers' awareness programmes were organized.

INTERNATIONAL ACTIVITIES AND TECHNICAL INFORMATION SERVICES
BIS in its capacity as the National Standards Body of India, is a member of International Organization
for Standardization (ISO) and through the Indian National Committee is a member of International
Electro-technical Commission (lEC). BIS actively participates in the activities of these International
bodies and is involved in development of International Standards as Participating (?) member or
Observer (0) member on various Technical Committees and Sub-Committees; and nominates technical
experts in Working Groups of these bodies.
As on 31 March 2021, BIS is a 'P' member in 482 Technical Committees/ Subcommittees of ISO and
98 Technical Committees/ Subcommittees of lEC, and '0' Member in 187 Technical Committees/
Subcommittees of ISO and 72 Technical Committees/ Subcommittees of lEC. BIS holds the Secretariat
of 11 important ISO Committees dealing with subjects that are of interest to India. Chairmanship of
"lEC Systems Committee on Low Voltage Direct Current and Low Voltage Direct Current for
Electricity Access" is held by India.
Convenorship of 26 ISO working groups and co-convenorship of 01 ISO working group is presently
with India.
BIS has entered into MoUs and BCAs with the National Standards Bodies of various countries for
cooperation in the areas of standardization, conformity assessment and training/ capacity building and
for mutual acceptance of each other's conformity assessment results respectively. BIS has so far signed
30 MoUs and 08 BCAs.
